Delivering a file is not the same as finishing the job.

GPT-6 Astra (with a low reasoning setting) finished and handed in a file on 211 of 220 tasks. The next step is making those files meet every requirement a professional would expect.

In plain English

GPT is very reliable at producing the requested file. But when the files are checked line by line against the task's checklist, they sometimes skip specific required details. A document can look polished and still be unusable at work if it misses a required safety step. The fix we suggest is training examples that teach the model to cover every stated requirement.

Example: a warehouse safety procedure

The task asked for an SOP (step-by-step procedure) for handling electronics that can be damaged by static electricity (ESD).

Both models delivered a Word document. Both judges scored Claude's at least 11.3 percentage points higher. Both judges found that GPT's document left out these requirements, which Claude's included:

  • Connecting the protected work area to a single shared ground point
  • Storing parts on grounded shelves or racks
  • Describing the symbol that marks static-protective packaging
  • Plain-language definitions of ESD and of parts that static can damage

We also read the original documents. GPT gave sound general advice on grounding but left out these specific requirements. See both files and every checklist verdict →

A similar pattern appeared in a journalism task: GPT delivered a Markdown text file instead of the requested PDF, and it left out context and figures the checklist required. These are individual examples; we have not measured how often this happens across all tasks.

Suggested training data: cover every requirement

A training dataset proposal describes new examples that could teach the model to fix this weakness.

  • Tasks: professional instructions and input files with many specific requirements, such as procedures, reports and regulated documents.
  • Expert guidance: checklists from experts that link each requirement to the part of the file that satisfies it.
  • Near-misses: plausible but incomplete files, paired with expert corrections.
  • Automatic checks: format and content rules a computer can verify where possible.
"Your model already delivers files reliably. The opportunity is making them operationally complete: covering the specific requirements that separate a plausible document from a usable one."

How to tell it worked: experts find fewer critical omissions on new tasks, and the model still delivers files just as often.

GPT ran with a low reasoning setting; a higher setting might change these results. Quality results come from AI judges and are provisional. Meeting the checklist does not certify that a document is technically safe.
