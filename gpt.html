Case study: warehouse electrostatic-discharge SOP

Both models delivered a Word SOP. Both judges favored Claude by at least 11.3 percentage points. Each judge independently recorded these GPT omissions, which Claude's document addressed:

  • Explicit common-point grounding of the protected area
  • Grounded shelving or racks for storage
  • Description of the ESD protective-packaging symbol
  • Plain-language definitions of ESD and ESD-sensitive items

Inspection of the original documents confirmed GPT supplied sound general grounding guidance but omitted these specific requirements. See both artifacts and every rubric verdict →

A similar pattern appears in a journalism task: GPT delivered Markdown rather than the requested PDF and omitted rubric-required trade-retaliation context and figures. These are case examples, not a measured dataset-wide omission rate.

Dataset proposal: requirement-to-artifact completeness

  • Tasks: professional instructions and reference files with dense, specific requirements (SOPs, reports, regulated documents).
  • Supervision: expert checklists linking each requirement to the artifact section that satisfies it.
  • Negative examples: plausible but incomplete deliverables, with expert corrections.
  • Acceptance tests: machine-checkable format and content constraints where possible.
"Your model already delivers files reliably. The opportunity is making them operationally complete: covering the specific requirements that separate a plausible document from a usable one."

Success measure: fewer expert-confirmed critical omissions on unseen tasks, with no drop in delivery rate.

GPT ran with low reasoning effort; higher effort may change these results. Judge outcomes are provisional and judge-sensitive. Rubric compliance does not certify technical safety.
