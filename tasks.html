GDPval gold tasks

GDPval is a benchmark of realistic professional work. Each task asks a model to act as a named professional and produce a real work product, usually a file such as a spreadsheet, memo, slide deck, notebook or audio mix. The public gold set has 220 tasks. Every task in it was run once by each model in this study.

In plain English

Think of each task as a realistic work assignment handed to a new employee: a description of the job, sometimes some files to work from, and a request for a finished file. Experts also wrote a detailed grading checklist for each assignment, listing everything a good answer must include. The checklist, not the expert's own answer, is what the models are graded against.

Anatomy of a task

PromptA role, a business situation and explicit deliverable requirements. Median 307 words (range 100–1,064).
Reference filesInputs the professional would be handed: source spreadsheets, PDFs, contracts, audio stems. 125 of 220 tasks have them, up to 17 per task.
Gold deliverableAn expert-made answer. It shows what good looks like but is not what the model is scored against; 35 tasks have no gold file in the public release.
RubricA checklist of weighted criteria written by people. Each criterion is a concrete, checkable statement about the deliverable. This is what is scored.

What is evaluated

Scoring uses the checklist. An AI judge reads the task, the checklist and the model's files, then marks each item. The task score is the share of checklist points earned. Items range from simple mechanical checks to calls that need expert judgement:

Weights

Most criteria are worth 1 or 2 points. A task carries a mean of 77 positive points (range 22–234). 94 penalty criteria across 18 tasks subtract points for professional errors, such as double-booking contractors or clipping an audio master.

Verdicts

  • pass: the delivered evidence satisfies the criterion.
  • fail: the evidence shows it is not satisfied.
  • unverified: the judge could not confirm it from what it could inspect, for example audio quality or whether a notebook runs.
  • unassessed: no verdict recorded because that grading attempt did not complete.

Because unverified items are uncertain, scores are reported as lower and upper bounds rather than a single number. Two judges, GPT and Claude, grade independently and are never averaged. See Methods & limits.

Audit sample testing on anti-financial-crime metrics

Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services · Accountants and Auditors · task 83d10b06

You are an auditor and as part of an audit engagement, you are tasked with reviewing and testing the accuracy of reported Anti-Financial Crime Risk Metrics.
  • Inputs provided: Population v2.xlsx
  • Expert gold deliverable: Sample v2.xlsx
  • Rubric: 38 criteria, 63 positive points
First three rubric criteria
  • The submitted deliverable is an Excel workbook file whose basename is 'Sample' (accept .xlsx, .xls, or .xlsm). (+2)
  • The workbook contains a worksheet named exactly 'Sample Size Calculation' (case-insensitive, ignoring surrounding spaces). (+2)
  • The 'Sample Size Calculation' worksheet explicitly states a confidence level of 90% and a tolerable error (error rate) of 10%. (+2)

American option pricing framework in a notebook

Finance and Insurance · Financial and Investment Analysts · task c7d83f01

You are a Quantitative Researcher at a proprietary trading firm. Historically, your desk has focused on delta-one products, but there is now a strategic initiative to expand into single-name options trading.

  • Expert gold deliverable: AmericanOptionPricing.ipynb
  • Rubric: 43 criteria, 53 positive points
First three rubric criteria
  • Provides a Python notebook file (.ipynb) as the deliverable implementing the American option pricing framework (+2)
  • The notebook runs end-to-end in a fresh kernel (Run All) without exceptions (+2)
  • Implements at least three distinct American option pricing methods chosen from: binomial or trinomial tree, finite-difference PDE, Monte Carlo with early exercise (e.g., LSM), or an analytical American approximation (+2)
