Can AI do real professional work? We tested two leading models on 220 job tasks.

GPT handed in a file far more often. Whose files were better is still mostly unclear, because the AI graders often could not tell and sometimes disagreed.

New here? The 60-second version
  1. The tasks220 realistic assignments from 44 jobs, such as an auditor's spreadsheet or a lawyer's memo, from OpenAI's GDPval benchmark. See the tasks →
  2. The modelsEach task was given once to GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI) and once to Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), with the same tools and the same limits.
  3. The outputEach model had to produce the actual work file, such as a Word document or Excel workbook. We call this the artifact.
  4. The gradingAI judges checked every file against a human-written checklist for that task. Both models served as judges.

New to the vocabulary? Underlined words link to the glossary. To see one task end to end, open the worked example.

Key results in plain English

  • GPT almost always delivered a file: 211 of 220 tasks, compared with 144 for Claude.
  • Claude often ran out of room: on 62 tasks Claude was still working when it hit the limits on steps or text length. Given more room in a follow-up test, it finished 54 of those 62.
  • On quality, there is no clear overall winner yet. On the tasks where both judges agreed, Claude's work was better on 29 and GPT's on 17. On most tasks, the judges could not separate the two.
Keep in mind: this compares two specific model settings, one try per task, graded by AI rather than human experts. Treat the quality results as provisional.

2. Whose work was better?

Each file was graded twice, once by a GPT judge and once by a Claude judge, without being told which model made it. For each task we compare the two models' scores. Because judges sometimes cannot check an item, each score is a range. If the two ranges overlap, the task is unresolved: we cannot say which model did better.

4. Follow-up: giving Claude more room

In a separate retry study, we re-ran Claude's 62 cut-off tasks with roughly two to three times the limits. Claude finished 54 of them, and 53 included a file. This suggests the limits explain much of the gap, but several settings changed at once, and the retry files have not been graded. These results do not replace the main results.
